### Heart anatomy, pericardium, and coronary supply

1. A 46-year-old woman with long-standing rheumatic mitral stenosis reports progressive difficulty swallowing solid food. Barium swallow shows extrinsic compression of the mid-esophagus. Enlargement of which cardiac chamber best explains this finding?
   - **A.** Right ventricle — The RV is the most anterior chamber, lying behind the sternum, far from the esophagus.
   - **B.** Right atrium — The RA forms the right heart border and lies away from the esophagus; it is not the chamber that compresses it.
   - **C.** Left atrium **(Correct)** — Correct. The LA is the most posterior part of the heart and sits directly in front of the esophagus; mitral stenosis enlarges it and can cause dysphagia.
   - **D.** Ascending aorta — The ascending aorta lies anteriorly and is not affected by mitral stenosis.
   - **E.** Left ventricle — The LV forms the apex and much of the inferior surface; it does not typically compress the esophagus.
   - Page: 287

2. The same patient later develops a hoarse voice. Laryngoscopy shows left vocal cord paralysis. Compression of which structure by the enlarged chamber explains her hoarseness?
   - **A.** Left recurrent laryngeal nerve **(Correct)** — Correct. An enlarged LA can compress the left recurrent laryngeal nerve (a vagal branch), causing hoarseness, known as Ortner syndrome.
   - **B.** Sympathetic chain — Sympathetic chain compression causes Horner features, not hoarseness.
   - **C.** Superior laryngeal nerve — The superior laryngeal nerve arises high in the neck and is not near the heart.
   - **D.** Phrenic nerve — The phrenic nerve supplies the diaphragm and pericardium; its compression does not cause hoarseness.
   - **E.** Right recurrent laryngeal nerve — The right recurrent laryngeal nerve loops under the right subclavian artery, away from the left atrium.
   - Page: 287

3. The eponym for hoarseness caused by cardiac chamber enlargement compressing a vagal branch is:
   - **A.** Beck triad — Beck triad (hypotension, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds) describes cardiac tamponade.
   - **B.** Horner syndrome — Horner syndrome results from interruption of sympathetic fibers to the face and eye.
   - **C.** Ortner syndrome **(Correct)** — Correct. Ortner syndrome is hoarseness from left recurrent laryngeal nerve compression, classically by an enlarged left atrium.
   - **D.** Eisenmenger syndrome — Eisenmenger syndrome is reversal of a left-to-right shunt due to pulmonary hypertension.
   - **E.** Kartagener syndrome — Kartagener syndrome is a ciliary dynein defect with dextrocardia and situs inversus.
   - Page: 287

4. A 22-year-old man is stabbed just left of the sternum at the 4th intercostal space. Which cardiac chamber is most likely to be injured?
   - **A.** Left atrium — The LA is the most posterior chamber, shielded by the rest of the heart.
   - **B.** Coronary sinus — The coronary sinus lies in the posterior AV groove, far from an anterior stab.
   - **C.** Right atrium — The RA forms the right border and is less exposed anteriorly than the RV.
   - **D.** Left ventricle — The LV lies more left-lateral and posterior; it is injured less often than the RV in anterior trauma.
   - **E.** Right ventricle **(Correct)** — Correct. The RV is the most anterior part of the heart and is the chamber most commonly injured in trauma.
   - Page: 287

5. On a cardiac CT, the inferior (diaphragmatic) surface of the heart is assessed. Approximately what proportion of this surface is formed by the left ventricle?

   - **B.** One-third — About one-third is formed by the RV, not the LV.
   - **C.** None of it — The LV is the major contributor to the diaphragmatic surface.
   - **D.** Two-thirds **(Correct)** — Correct. The LV forms about 2/3 and the RV about 1/3 of the inferior cardiac surface.
   - **E.** All of it — The RV also contributes to the diaphragmatic surface.
   - Page: 287

6. Which sequence correctly lists the layers of the pericardium from outermost to innermost?
   - **A.** Parietal pericardium → fibrous pericardium → epicardium — The fibrous layer is the outermost; parietal serous pericardium lies inside it.
   - **B.** Fibrous pericardium → parietal pericardium → epicardium **(Correct)** — Correct. Outer to inner: fibrous pericardium, parietal pericardium, then epicardium (visceral pericardium).
   - **C.** Epicardium → parietal pericardium → fibrous pericardium — This lists the layers from inside out.
   - **D.** Fibrous pericardium → epicardium → parietal pericardium — The epicardium is the innermost pericardial layer, lying on the myocardium.
   - **E.** Parietal pericardium → epicardium → fibrous pericardium — The fibrous pericardium cannot be the innermost layer.
   - Page: 287

7. During pericardiocentesis, the needle enters the space that normally contains a thin film of serous fluid. This pericardial space lies between which two layers?
   - **A.** Myocardium and endocardium — These are layers of the heart wall itself, not the pericardium.
   - **B.** Epicardium and myocardium — The epicardium adheres directly to the myocardium.
   - **C.** Parietal pericardium and epicardium **(Correct)** — Correct. The pericardial space lies between the parietal pericardium and the epicardium (visceral pericardium).
   - **D.** Fibrous pericardium and mediastinal pleura — This is outside the pericardial sac.
   - **E.** Fibrous pericardium and parietal pericardium — These two layers are fused; there is no potential space between them.
   - Page: 287

8. A 30-year-old man with viral pericarditis has sharp chest pain that radiates to his left shoulder. Which nerve carries the sensory fibers responsible for this referred pain?
   - **A.** Phrenic nerve **(Correct)** — Correct. The pericardium is innervated by the phrenic nerve (C3–C5), so pericarditis can refer pain to the neck, arms, or shoulders.
   - **B.** Greater splanchnic nerve — Splanchnic nerves carry visceral afferents from abdominal organs.
   - **C.** Long thoracic nerve — The long thoracic nerve supplies serratus anterior and carries no pericardial sensation.
   - **D.** Vagus nerve — The vagus carries visceral afferents but does not explain shoulder-tip referral.
   - **E.** Intercostal nerves — Intercostal nerves supply the chest wall, not the pericardium.
   - Page: 287

9. Which coronary artery is most commonly occluded in myocardial infarction?

   - **B.** Right coronary artery — The RCA is commonly involved but is second to the LAD.
   - **C.** Left circumflex artery — The LCX is less frequently occluded than the LAD.
   - **D.** Posterior descending artery — The PDA is a terminal branch and less often the primary site of occlusion.
   - **E.** Acute marginal artery — The acute marginal artery supplies the RV and is rarely the culprit.

10. A 58-year-old man has an MI involving the anterior 2/3 of the interventricular septum and the anterior wall of the LV. Which papillary muscle is also supplied by the occluded vessel?

   - **C.** Posteromedial papillary muscle — The posteromedial papillary muscle is supplied by the PDA.
   - **D.** Septal papillary muscle of the RV — This is not a territory listed for the LAD and is not what this question targets.

   - **C.** Posteromedial; left anterior descending — The LAD supplies the anterior septum and anterior LV, not the posteromedial muscle.
   - **D.** Anterolateral; right coronary artery — The RCA does not supply the anterolateral papillary muscle.
   - **E.** Anterolateral; left anterior descending — The anterolateral papillary muscle is in LAD territory, not affected by an inferior MI.

13. A 70-year-old man with an acute inferior MI becomes bradycardic with complete heart block. Involvement of which artery best explains the conduction abnormality in a right-dominant heart?
   - **A.** Obtuse marginal artery — The obtuse marginal supplies the lateral LV wall.
   - **B.** Right coronary artery via the PDA **(Correct)** — Correct. In right-dominant hearts the PDA arises from the RCA and supplies the SA and AV nodes; infarction can cause bradycardia or heart block.
   - **C.** Left circumflex artery — In a right-dominant heart the LCX does not give rise to the PDA.
   - **D.** Left main coronary artery — Left main occlusion causes extensive anterior/lateral infarction, not isolated nodal ischemia.
   - **E.** Left anterior descending artery — The LAD supplies the anterior septum and LV, not the nodes in this pattern.

14. Coronary dominance is defined by the origin of which vessel?

   - **D.** Coronary sinus — The coronary sinus is a vein and does not define dominance.
   - **E.** Posterior descending artery **(Correct)** — Correct. Dominance is determined by which vessel gives rise to the PDA.

   - **C.** Anomalous origin, always pathologic — Left dominance is a normal anatomic variant.
   - **D.** Right dominant — Right dominance means the PDA arises from the RCA.
   - **E.** Codominant — Codominance means the PDA arises from both the LCX and RCA.

17. Blood flow through the coronary arteries supplying the left ventricle and interventricular septum is greatest during which phase of the cardiac cycle?

23. A 9-year-old boy has chronic sinusitis, recurrent bronchiectasis, and heart sounds loudest on the right side of the chest. A defect in which protein explains his cardiac position?
