### Pituitary, adrenal zones, and pancreatic islets

1. A 30-year-old woman has headaches, amenorrhea and galactorrhea. MRI shows a sellar mass arising from the adenohypophysis. From which embryologic tissue does this part of the pituitary derive?
   - **A.** Endoderm — Endoderm forms gut-derived glands such as thyroid follicles and pancreas.
   - **B.** Mesoderm — Mesoderm forms the adrenal cortex.
   - **C.** Neural crest — Neural crest forms the adrenal medulla.
   - **D.** Oral ectoderm (Rathke pouch) **(Correct)** — Correct. The anterior pituitary derives from oral ectoderm via Rathke pouch.
   - **E.** Neuroectoderm — Neuroectoderm forms the posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis).
   - Page: 331

2. Which hormone is NOT secreted by the anterior pituitary?
   - **A.** Oxytocin **(Correct)** — Correct. Oxytocin is made in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary.
   - **B.** TSH — TSH is an anterior pituitary basophil hormone.
   - **C.** Prolactin — Prolactin is secreted by anterior pituitary acidophils.
   - **D.** ACTH — ACTH is secreted by anterior pituitary basophils.
   - **E.** Growth hormone — GH is secreted by anterior pituitary acidophils.
   - Page: 331

3. Vasopressin and oxytocin are synthesized in which location?
   - **A.** Pineal gland — The pineal gland makes melatonin.
   - **B.** Arcuate nucleus — The arcuate nucleus makes dopamine and GHRH.
   - **C.** Supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus **(Correct)** — Correct. ADH and oxytocin are made in hypothalamic nuclei and carried to the posterior pituitary by neurophysins.
   - **D.** Posterior pituitary — The posterior pituitary stores and releases them but does not synthesize them.
   - **E.** Anterior pituitary — The anterior pituitary does not make these hormones.
   - Page: 331

4. The carrier proteins that transport ADH and oxytocin down axons to the posterior pituitary are called:
   - **A.** Transcortins — Transcortin (CBG) binds cortisol in plasma.
   - **B.** Thyroglobulins — Thyroglobulin is the thyroid hormone storage protein.
   - **C.** Neurophysins **(Correct)** — Correct. Neurophysins carry ADH and oxytocin from hypothalamic nuclei to the posterior pituitary.
   - **D.** Synaptophysins — Synaptophysin is a synaptic vesicle protein used as a neuroendocrine marker.
   - **E.** Chromogranins — Chromogranin is a marker of neuroendocrine secretory granules.
   - Page: 331

5. A woman has a positive urine pregnancy test, but the lab notes that a nonspecific assay could cross-react with LH, FSH and TSH. Which shared feature explains the cross-reactivity?
   - **A.** They share a common α subunit; the β subunit confers specificity **(Correct)** — Correct. TSH, LH, FSH and hCG share the α subunit; the β subunit determines specificity, so specific tests measure β-hCG.
   - **B.** They are all derived from POMC — POMC gives ACTH, MSH and β-endorphin.
   - **C.** They are all secreted by acidophils — TSH, LH and FSH are basophil products; hCG is placental.
   - **D.** All are steroid hormones — These are glycoprotein hormones, not steroids.
   - **E.** They all bind the same receptor — Each has a distinct receptor.
   - Page: 331

6. Proopiomelanocortin (POMC) is the precursor of which group of hormones?
   - **A.** CRH and GnRH — These are hypothalamic releasing hormones.
   - **B.** TSH, LH and FSH — These are glycoproteins sharing an α subunit.
   - **C.** ADH and oxytocin — These are hypothalamic nonapeptides.
   - **D.** ACTH, MSH and β-endorphin **(Correct)** — Correct. POMC yields β-endorphin, ACTH and MSH ("go pro with a BAM").
   - **E.** GH, prolactin and hPL — These are structurally related but not POMC derivatives.
   - Page: 331

7. A pituitary section is stained with H&E. Which pair of hormones is made by acidophilic (eosinophilic) cells?
   - **A.** MSH and β-endorphin — These are POMC products of basophils and the intermediate lobe.
   - **B.** FSH and LH — These come from basophils.
   - **C.** ADH and oxytocin — These are released from the posterior pituitary, which is neural tissue.
   - **D.** ACTH and TSH — These come from basophils.
   - **E.** Prolactin and GH **(Correct)** — Correct. "Acid PiG": acidophils make PRL and GH.

8. Which anterior pituitary hormones are produced by basophils?
   - **A.** GH and prolactin — These are acidophil hormones.
   - **B.** Calcitonin and PTH — These come from the thyroid and parathyroid.
   - **C.** FSH, LH, ACTH and TSH **(Correct)** — Correct. "B-FLAT": basophils make FSH, LH, ACTH and TSH.
   - **D.** Only ACTH — ACTH is one of four basophil hormones.
   - **E.** ADH and oxytocin — These come from the hypothalamus via the posterior pituitary.
   - Page: 331

9. Melanotropin (MSH) is secreted mainly from which part of the pituitary?
   - **A.** Median eminence — The median eminence is the hypothalamic site of releasing-hormone secretion into portal blood.
   - **B.** Intermediate lobe **(Correct)** — Correct. MSH is secreted from the intermediate lobe of the pituitary.
   - **C.** Pars nervosa — The pars nervosa is the posterior pituitary.
   - **D.** Pars tuberalis — The pars tuberalis wraps the infundibulum and is not the main MSH source.
   - **E.** Infundibulum — The infundibulum is the pituitary stalk.
   - Page: 331

10. The posterior pituitary derives from which embryologic tissue?
   - **A.** Endoderm — Endoderm forms thyroid follicles and pancreatic islets.

   - **C.** Oral ectoderm — Oral ectoderm forms the anterior pituitary.
   - **D.** Neuroectoderm **(Correct)** — Correct. The neurohypophysis is a downgrowth of the diencephalon (neuroectoderm).

11. A 52-year-old man has hypertension and hypokalemia. Imaging shows an adrenal adenoma arising from the outermost zone of the cortex. What is the main physiologic regulator of this zone?

   - **D.** Angiotensin II **(Correct)** — Correct. The zona glomerulosa, which makes aldosterone, is regulated mainly by angiotensin II (and K+).

12. Which adrenal zone produces cortisol?

   - **E.** Capsule — The capsule is connective tissue and makes no hormones.
   - Page: 331

13. A useful mnemonic for the adrenal cortex layers from outside in is "GFR = salt, sugar, sex." Which hormone corresponds to the innermost cortical layer?

17. In the islets of Langerhans, insulin-secreting cells are typically located:

18. A 58-year-old man has migratory necrolytic erythema and new diabetes. A tumor of which islet cell type is most likely, and where are these cells located in a normal islet?

   - **D.** α cells; center — α cells are peripheral.
   - **E.** α cells; periphery **(Correct)** — Correct. Glucagonoma arises from α cells, which sit at the islet periphery.

19. Which islet cell secretes somatostatin?

### Thyroid development, thyroglossal duct, and ectopic thyroid

21. A 6-year-old girl has a painless 1.5-cm mass in the anterior midline of the neck just below the hyoid bone. The mass rises when she protrudes her tongue. What is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **A.** Cystic hygroma — Cystic hygromas are lymphatic malformations, usually in the posterior triangle, and do not move with the tongue.
   - **B.** Thyroglossal duct cyst **(Correct)** — Correct. A persistent thyroglossal duct forms an anterior midline cyst that moves with swallowing or tongue protrusion because the tract is attached to the tongue base.
   - **C.** Dermoid cyst — A dermoid can be midline but does not elevate with tongue protrusion.
   - **D.** Branchial (pharyngeal) cleft cyst — Pharyngeal cleft cysts lie in the lateral neck, anterior to the sternocleidomastoid, and do not move with tongue protrusion.
   - **E.** Thyroid follicular adenoma — A thyroid nodule moves with swallowing but sits lower, in the gland, and does not rise with tongue protrusion.
   - Page: 330

22. From which embryologic structure does the thyroid diverticulum arise?
   - **A.** Roof of the primitive pharynx — The thyroid begins on the floor, not the roof, of the primitive pharynx.
   - **B.** Floor of the primitive pharynx **(Correct)** — Correct. The thyroid diverticulum arises from the floor of the primitive pharynx and descends into the neck.
   - **C.** 3rd pharyngeal pouch — The 3rd pouch forms the thymus and inferior parathyroids.
   - **D.** Neural crest — Neural crest gives rise to parafollicular C cells in some schemes, but the gland itself starts as a pharyngeal floor diverticulum.
   - **E.** Rathke pouch — Rathke pouch, from oral ectoderm, forms the anterior pituitary.
   - Page: 330

23. The normal adult remnant of the thyroglossal duct, seen as a small pit on the dorsum of the tongue, is the:
   - **A.** Lingual tonsil — The lingual tonsil is lymphoid tissue, unrelated to thyroid descent.
   - **B.** Sulcus terminalis — The sulcus terminalis is the V-shaped groove behind which the foramen cecum lies; it is not itself the duct remnant.
   - **C.** Foramen cecum **(Correct)** — Correct. The foramen cecum is the normal remnant of the thyroglossal duct at the tongue base.
   - **D.** Vallate papillae — Vallate papillae are taste structures anterior to the sulcus terminalis.
   - **E.** Pyramidal lobe — A pyramidal lobe is a persistent distal thyroglossal duct remnant, not the normal tongue pit.
   - Page: 330

24. A 34-year-old woman undergoes thyroidectomy. The surgeon notes a thin superior extension of thyroid tissue rising from the isthmus toward the hyoid. This structure most likely represents:
   - **A.** A metastatic lymph node — A node would not be a continuous lobe of normal thyroid tissue.
   - **B.** A persistent distal thyroglossal duct (pyramidal lobe) **(Correct)** — Correct. The thyroglossal duct can persist as cysts or as the pyramidal lobe of the thyroid.
   - **C.** A 2nd pharyngeal cleft remnant — 2nd cleft remnants lie laterally along the sternocleidomastoid.
   - **D.** An ectopic thymic remnant — Thymic remnants descend toward the mediastinum, not up to the hyoid.
   - **E.** A parathyroid adenoma — Parathyroid tissue sits on the posterior thyroid surface, not as a superior midline extension.
   - Page: 330

25. A 9-year-old boy has a firm, round, reddish mass at the base of his tongue causing dysphagia. Thyroid scintigraphy shows uptake only in the mass, with no tissue in the neck. What is the most important risk of surgically removing the mass?
   - **A.** Permanent hypothyroidism **(Correct)** — Correct. A lingual thyroid may be the only functioning thyroid tissue; removing it causes hypothyroidism.
   - **B.** Hypoparathyroidism — Parathyroids develop from pharyngeal pouches and descend into the neck separately.
   - **C.** Thyroid storm — Thyroid storm is a hyperthyroid crisis, not a consequence of removing the only thyroid tissue.
   - **D.** Hypercalcemia — Removing thyroid tissue does not cause hypercalcemia.
   - **E.** Recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy — The lingual mass is far from the recurrent laryngeal nerves.
   - Page: 330

26. What is the most common site of ectopic thyroid tissue?
   - **A.** Ovary — Struma ovarii is thyroid tissue in an ovarian teratoma, a rare site.
   - **B.** Mediastinum — Mediastinal thyroid is usually a substernal goiter extension, less common than lingual thyroid.
   - **C.** Submandibular gland — This is not a recognized common site of ectopic thyroid.
   - **D.** Lateral neck — Lateral neck thyroid tissue suggests metastatic thyroid cancer until proven otherwise.
   - **E.** Tongue **(Correct)** — Correct. The tongue (lingual thyroid) is the most common site of ectopic thyroid tissue.
   - Page: 330

27. A child has a midline neck mass. Which physical finding best distinguishes a thyroglossal duct cyst from a pharyngeal cleft cyst?
   - **A.** Size greater than 2 cm — Size does not distinguish the two.
   - **B.** Tenderness on palpation — Both cysts can become tender if infected.
   - **C.** Movement with swallowing or tongue protrusion in the midline **(Correct)** — Correct. Thyroglossal duct cysts are midline and move with swallowing or tongue protrusion; pharyngeal cleft cysts are lateral and fixed.
   - **D.** Presence of a sinus opening on the skin — Sinus tracts can occur with cleft anomalies but are not the defining difference.
   - **E.** Transillumination — Both fluid-filled cysts may transilluminate.
   - Page: 330

28. A 25-year-old man has a painless mass in the lateral neck anterior to the sternocleidomastoid that does not move with swallowing. What is the most likely origin?
   - **A.** 4th pharyngeal pouch — 4th pouch derivatives are the superior parathyroids and parafollicular cells, not lateral neck cysts.
   - **B.** Thyroglossal duct — A thyroglossal duct cyst is midline and moves with swallowing.
   - **C.** Persistent cervical sinus (pharyngeal cleft) **(Correct)** — Correct. A persistent cervical sinus leads to a pharyngeal cleft cyst in the lateral neck.
   - **D.** Lingual thyroid — Lingual thyroid is at the tongue base.
   - **E.** Pyramidal lobe — The pyramidal lobe is midline, rising from the isthmus.
   - Page: 330

29. Thyroid follicular cells, which make thyroglobulin and T4, are derived from which germ layer?

   - **C.** 1st pharyngeal cleft — The 1st cleft forms the external auditory meatus.
   - **D.** Floor of the primitive pharynx — The pharyngeal floor gives rise to follicular cells.
   - **E.** 2nd pharyngeal pouch — The 2nd pouch forms the epithelial lining of the palatine tonsil.
   - Page: 330

31. During embryonic descent, the developing thyroid remains attached to the tongue by which structure?

32. A 7-year-old has a midline neck cyst excised. To minimize recurrence, the surgeon removes the tract along with the central portion of which structure?

   - **C.** Thyroid cartilage — The tract passes near, but the bone excised in the Sistrunk procedure is the hyoid.

34. A newborn has congenital hypothyroidism on screening. Imaging finds a small round focus of thyroid tissue at the base of the tongue and no gland in the neck. Which developmental process failed?

35. A 40-year-old woman has medullary thyroid carcinoma. Which serum marker reflects the embryologic cell of origin of this tumor?

### SIADH, diabetes insipidus, and primary polydipsia

41. A 64-year-old smoker has confusion. Serum Na+ 118 mEq/L, serum osmolality 250 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality 520 mOsm/kg, urine Na+ 60 mEq/L. He appears euvolemic. What is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **A.** Primary polydipsia — Primary polydipsia produces maximally dilute urine.
   - **B.** Adrenal insufficiency with hypovolemia — He is euvolemic, and hypovolemia would lower urine Na+.
   - **C.** SIADH from ectopic ADH secretion **(Correct)** — Correct. Euvolemic hyponatremia with urine osm > serum osm and continued urinary Na+ excretion is SIADH; small cell lung cancer is a classic ectopic source.
   - **D.** Central diabetes insipidus — DI causes hypernatremia and dilute urine.
   - **E.** Cirrhosis — Cirrhosis causes hypervolemic hyponatremia with low urine Na+.
   - Page: 342

42. In SIADH, why do patients remain clinically euvolemic despite retaining free water?
   - **A.** Volume expansion lowers aldosterone and raises ANP/BNP, increasing urinary Na+ excretion **(Correct)** — Correct. These responses normalize extracellular volume, producing euvolemic hyponatremia.
   - **B.** Increased thirst is suppressed — Not the main mechanism.
   - **C.** The retained water stays inside cells only — Not the explanation.
   - **D.** ADH causes renal sodium retention — ADH retains water, not sodium.
   - **E.** Edema hides the extra volume — Patients are not edematous.
   - Page: 342

43. What is the first-line treatment for asymptomatic SIADH?
   - **A.** Fluid restriction **(Correct)** — Correct. Fluid restriction is first line.
   - **B.** IV hypertonic saline — Reserved for severe or symptomatic hyponatremia.
   - **C.** Desmopressin — Desmopressin would worsen water retention.
   - **D.** Hydrochlorothiazide — Thiazides worsen hyponatremia.
   - **E.** Normal saline infusion — Isotonic saline can worsen hyponatremia in SIADH.
   - Page: 342

44. A patient with refractory SIADH is started on an oral drug that blocks V2 receptors. Which drug is it?
   - **A.** Spironolactone — An aldosterone antagonist.
   - **B.** Fludrocortisone — A mineralocorticoid, not an ADH antagonist.
   - **C.** Mannitol — An osmotic diuretic.
   - **D.** Tolvaptan **(Correct)** — Correct. Tolvaptan (and conivaptan) are ADH antagonists used for SIADH.
   - **E.** Desmopressin — Desmopressin is a V2 agonist.
   - Page: 342

45. Which drug is a recognized cause of SIADH?
   - **A.** Lithium — Lithium causes nephrogenic DI.
   - **B.** Demeclocycline — Demeclocycline treats SIADH and causes nephrogenic DI.
   - **C.** Ethanol — Ethanol suppresses ADH.
   - **D.** Carbamazepine **(Correct)** — Correct. SSRIs, carbamazepine and cyclophosphamide are drug causes of SIADH.
   - **E.** Phenytoin — Phenytoin inhibits ADH release.
